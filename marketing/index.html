The product tour

Follow a trip through the system.

From the schedule feed to a broker's phone, every screen below is the real application — the same components your crews use in the cockpit and your dispatchers use on the desk.

Dispatch · Ops Console

Run the day from one board.

The Ops Console pulls today's trips plus anything still in progress into a single card grid, each with a live eight-step status strip. Skyway flags what's missing before it bites — no trip sheet, no broker email, unparsed passengers, no PIC or SIC assigned — and pushes updates straight to the crew's phones.

  • Filters for with flags and in progress, plus totals at a glance
  • Schedule syncs from a JetInsight iCal feed or manual entry
  • Send-update pushes to assigned dispatchers via Firebase Cloud Messaging
Crew · Trip status

Eight taps from onsite to landing.

On each trip the crew works a status timeline — crew onsite (with a GPS lock at the FBO), aircraft ready, catering aboard, passengers arrived and boarded, taxi, wheels up, landed. Every step can fire a branded broker email automatically, and FlightAware confirms the airborne and landing events on its own.

  • Per-leg weather, NOTAMs, FBOs and crew on the same screen
  • Tail status badge surfaces open squawks and MEL deferrals at acceptance
  • Repositioning legs get their own status language automatically
Flights · Live tracking

The whole fleet, moving in real time.

Skyway polls every tail through FlightAware AeroAPI and draws each aircraft on a shared map with an altitude-colored trail, projected path, ETA, and origin and destination METARs. Parked aircraft show at their last landing airport. The same map engine renders the TV flight board and the public broker page.

  • Dark, satellite and terrain basemaps with a weather-radar overlay
  • Positions refresh every 30 seconds; full track logs on demand
  • No Mapbox token required — Carto, Esri and OpenTopoMap tiles
Crew · Duty & rest

A duty engine that reads the reg.

Skyway evaluates every duty period against 14 CFR 135.267 — the 10-hour rest requirement, the 14-hour duty limit, the extended-rest tiers after a flight-time excursion, and the quarterly 13×24-hour rest rule from §135.265(c). Legality is shown as LEGAL / WARNING / ILLEGAL, and the administrator report rolls it up across the fleet with a full audit trail.

  • Paired-crew flow: PIC starts duty, SIC confirms fit-for-duty
  • 365 days of records retained; corrections are audited, never deleted
  • Export to CSV or PDF for the ops manual and the POI
Brokers & clients · Share link

Give the broker a link, not your ops data.

One tap mints an HMAC-signed public tracking page. Brokers and clients see the tail, itinerary, live map, weather and status timeline — and nothing else. No pricing, no crew contacts, no internal notes. Rotate or revoke the link anytime; it expires 24 hours after the last leg lands.

  • Works on any device, no login — premium or classic broker themes
  • Optional passenger check-in status, only when you enable it
  • Same live position and trail your dispatchers see

Regulatory engine

Purpose-built for unscheduled Part 135.

Skyway's legality engine encodes the actual rules charter crews fly under — with the citation attached to each one. It is conservative by design: it prefers a warning over a silent pass, and every interpretation is meant for your Director of Operations and Chief Pilot to review.

14 CFR §135.267(b)(3)
10 consecutive hours of rest

Required in the 24 hours before a planned assignment completes — evaluated on every duty period.

14 CFR §135.267(c)
14-hour duty period limit

The regular-assignment duty cap, flagged as crews approach and exceed it.

14 CFR §135.267(d)
Extended rest after an excursion

Tiered make-up rest — 11, 12, or 16 hours — when a prior period ran past its flight-time limit.

14 CFR §135.265(c)
13 × 24-hour rest per quarter

Tracks the thirteen 24-hour rest periods required each calendar quarter.

14 CFR §61.57
Takeoff, night & instrument recency

Auto-computed from assigned trips — night is judged from local sunrise/sunset at each airport.

§135.293 / .297 / .299 / .351
The Part 135 check cycle

Competency checks, instrument proficiency, line checks and recurrent training, with 60/30/14-day alerts.

Crew · Command Center

Legality, live on the ops board.

The crew duty board shows who is on duty, resting, available, or approaching a limit in real time — with elapsed duty, seat, and the trailing 7/30/90-day workload for each pilot. Administrators drill into a timeline editor, link PIC and SIC periods, and export the official record without leaving the screen.

  • Trailing-window duty and flight-hour aggregates per pilot
  • Illegal periods are flagged but never block a submission — override is audited
  • JetInsight duty history and 24-column currency grids import in bulk

Questions

What operators ask first.

Who is Skyway Ops for?

Business and corporate aviation flight departments operating under Part 91 and Part 135 — charter operators, management companies, and owner-flown fleets that need dispatch, tracking, duty and rest, maintenance, and back-office in one place.

Do we have to replace our scheduling software?

No. Skyway syncs your existing JetInsight schedule through an iCal feed and layers operations on top. You can also add trips by hand for anything the feed doesn't carry.

How does the duty engine handle our specific operation?

The engine encodes 14 CFR §135.267 for unscheduled operations plus the quarterly rest rule from §135.265(c), with the citation attached to each limit. It is conservative and meant to be reviewed by your Director of Operations and Chief Pilot against your ops specs — it is a compliance aid, not a legal authority.

What about our maintenance system of record?

Skyway coordinates squawks, MEL deferrals, the AML, and AOG events, and projects due dates from a Veryon due-list import. Your maintenance-tracking provider stays authoritative for airworthiness; Skyway is the operational layer around it.

Does it work on a phone in the cockpit?

Yes. Skyway Ops is a progressive web app that installs on iOS and Android, syncs in real time, and is designed for use on the ramp — including GPS-locked crew-onsite check-in and push notifications with quiet hours.

How do crews sign in?

Through Microsoft Entra ID single sign-on with your company accounts. New users are provisioned with crew access pending administrator approval, and every role is scoped to what that job needs.
